# Research Report Writing & Editorial Standards

Internal publication standard for company-authored research reports published on the official company website and public repositories.

This policy defines the reference-quality requirements, formal academic writing standards, and editorial quality gates applicable to all research publications, white papers, and technical reports authored by earthoslab.

---

## 1. Formal Academic Writing

- Avoid first-person pronouns such as "I", "we", "our", "us", and "my". Use objective, impersonal constructions.
- Maintain a formal, professional, neutral, and evidence-driven tone throughout the report.
- Avoid conversational language, slang, informal expressions, contractions, and rhetorical questions.
- Avoid promotional, persuasive, emotional, or marketing-oriented language.
- Do not use exaggerated adjectives such as "revolutionary", "groundbreaking", "remarkable", "unprecedented", or "exceptional" without measurable justification.
- Prefer precise and direct sentences over unnecessarily complicated or decorative language.
- Avoid vague expressions such as "very effective", "highly accurate", "significantly better", and "considerably improved" without quantitative evidence.
- Avoid subjective judgments, personal opinions, and unsupported interpretations.
- Use complete grammatical sentences and maintain proper subject-verb agreement.
- Maintain consistent verb tense: past tense for completed experiments, present tense for established knowledge and current observations, and future tense for explicitly proposed work.
- Avoid unnecessary passive voice, but use it where the research process or result is more important than the person performing the action.
- Avoid excessive use of nominalizations and unnecessarily abstract expressions.
- Avoid sentence fragments, run-on sentences, and comma splices.
- Ensure pronouns have unambiguous antecedents.
- Avoid using "etc.", "and so on", and similar incomplete expressions in formal technical descriptions.
- Avoid contractions such as "don't", "can't", "isn't", and "won't".
- Avoid casual transitions such as "Anyway", "Obviously", "Basically", and "As we all know".
- Avoid using "clearly", "obviously", or "evidently" as substitutes for actual explanation.
- Ensure every sentence communicates a meaningful technical or contextual point.

---

## 2. Terminology and Technical Consistency

- Use one consistent term for each technical concept throughout the report.
- If a component is introduced as a "model", always call it a "model". Do not alternate between "model", "framework", "architecture", "system", and "approach" without a genuine technical distinction.
- Maintain consistent naming conventions for algorithms, modules, datasets, variables, experimental configurations, and evaluation metrics.
- Define abbreviations at first occurrence and use them consistently afterward.
- Avoid introducing unnecessary abbreviations.
- Maintain consistent capitalization, spelling, hyphenation, and notation.
- Use consistent measurement units, numerical precision, and mathematical symbols.
- Ensure terminology in text, figures, tables, equations, and supplementary materials matches exactly.
- Do not introduce alternative names merely to avoid repeating a technical term.
- Maintain consistent use of singular and plural forms for technical entities.

---

## 3. Paragraph and Sentence Construction

- Prefer coherent, developed paragraphs over excessive bullet points.
- Each paragraph should communicate one primary idea or argument.
- Begin paragraphs with a clear topic or contextual statement.
- Maintain logical progression between sentences and paragraphs.
- Use appropriate transitions to establish relationships between arguments.
- Avoid excessively long paragraphs containing multiple unrelated ideas.
- Avoid unnecessary one-sentence paragraphs.
- Avoid repeating information already explained in preceding paragraphs.
- Avoid presenting multiple independent arguments within one sentence.
- Use bullet points only when they improve the presentation of procedures, specifications, or genuinely separable information.
- Ensure paragraphs are connected logically rather than functioning as isolated statements.
- Maintain a consistent level of technical detail throughout each section.

---

## 4. Formal Vocabulary and Expression

- Prefer precise academic verbs such as "demonstrates", "indicates", "establishes", "evaluates", "measures", and "suggests" where appropriate.
- Avoid informal verbs such as "gets", "shows up", "figures out", "looks at", and "comes up with" in formal technical descriptions.
- Avoid unnecessarily complex vocabulary when a simpler technical expression communicates the same meaning.
- Do not use synonyms interchangeably when they represent different technical meanings.
- Avoid unnecessary intensifiers such as "extremely", "incredibly", "massively", and "tremendously".
- Use cautious language when evidence is limited. Distinguish "suggests", "indicates", and "demonstrates" according to the strength of evidence.
- Use technical terminology accurately rather than relying on sophisticated-sounding language.
- Avoid ambiguous expressions such as "this approach", "the above method", and "the same" when the reference is unclear.
- Expand abbreviations when necessary for clarity, especially at the beginning of independent sections.

---

## 5. Figures, Images, and Visual References

- Every figure must be explicitly referenced in the main text.
- Introduce or discuss figures before or near their placement.
- Use one consistent reference format, such as "Figure 1".
- Ensure figure numbering follows the order of first appearance.
- Keep figure captions short, precise, and informative.
- Captions should describe the figure without repeating the entire discussion.
- Explain the relevance and implications of important figures in the surrounding paragraphs.
- Ensure diagrams accurately represent the actual methodology, architecture, or experimental process.
- Maintain consistent typography, colors, dimensions, and visual styling.
- Ensure axes, legends, labels, and annotations are readable.
- Reference subfigures correctly, such as "Figure 2(a)".
- Avoid decorative imagery that does not contribute to technical understanding.
- Ensure all images have appropriate attribution or usage permissions.
- Do not leave figures without an explicit textual reference.

---

## 6. Tables and Equations

- Reference every table explicitly in the main text.
- Use concise, descriptive table captions.
- Maintain consistent alignment, units, borders, and numerical precision.
- Explain abbreviations and symbols used in tables.
- Avoid duplicating identical information in tables and paragraphs.
- Number equations sequentially.
- Reference numbered equations explicitly in the text.
- Define every mathematical variable and symbol.
- Maintain consistent mathematical notation throughout the report.
- Verify mathematical derivations and computational correctness.
- Ensure equations are properly formatted and readable in web and PDF versions.

---

## 7. References and Source Quality

### Mandatory Source-Quality Policy

- Do not use preprints as supporting references in the final published research report.
- Do not cite unpublished manuscripts, working papers, or manuscripts that have not completed formal publication or acceptance.
- Do not use arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, SSRN, or other preprint repositories as substitutes for peer-reviewed publications.
- If a preprint has subsequently been published in a peer-reviewed journal or conference, cite the final published version instead.
- Verify that the cited publication is genuinely published and not merely submitted, under review, or accepted but unpublished.
- Prefer peer-reviewed journal articles, peer-reviewed conference proceedings, established academic books, and authoritative institutional publications.
- Use primary research sources whenever possible rather than relying exclusively on review articles.
- Prioritize original research papers when citing experimental methods, findings, or established results.
- Avoid citing blogs, personal websites, forums, and commercial articles as scientific evidence.
- Use official documentation for software specifications, APIs, datasets, and technical implementations where peer-reviewed sources are unavailable.
- Use official government and institutional sources for statistical, regulatory, and public datasets.
- Verify the publication venue, authors, title, year, volume, issue, page numbers, and DOI.
- Ensure the DOI resolves to the actual cited publication.
- Ensure every in-text citation has a corresponding reference-list entry.
- Ensure every reference-list entry is cited in the report, except explicitly designated further reading.
- Never fabricate citations, publication details, URLs, or DOI information.
- Do not cite a source solely because another paper cited it. Verify the original source whenever possible.
- Do not use citations that only partially or indirectly support the associated claim.
- Distinguish established literature from the company's original findings.

> **Important Exception**: Preprints may be consulted internally for literature discovery, emerging research awareness, and identifying potential research gaps. However, they should not appear in the final report's reference list under this policy. If no formally published source supports an important claim, qualify the claim or disclose the lack of established evidence rather than disguising an unpublished source as published research.

## 8. Research Claims and Evidence

- Every significant technical claim must be supported by evidence, mathematical reasoning, or an appropriate citation.
- Distinguish measured results from theoretical expectations and hypotheses.
- Do not claim superiority without relevant and fair baseline comparisons.
- Avoid presenting correlation as causation.
- Document experimental assumptions, constraints, and boundary conditions.
- Ensure reported metrics match the original experimental outputs.
- Do not selectively report favorable findings while ignoring contradictory results.
- Acknowledge negative, inconclusive, and unexpected findings.
- Distinguish demonstrated capabilities from proposed capabilities.
- Do not generalize conclusions beyond the scope of the experiments.
- Do not imply external validation when only internal experiments have been conducted.
- Label simulations, synthetic datasets, preliminary results, and real-world observations accurately.
- Ensure statistical conclusions are supported by appropriate statistical analysis.
- Distinguish statistical significance from practical significance.
- Clearly identify assumptions behind theoretical claims.

---

## 9. Methodology and Reproducibility

- Explain the methodology sufficiently for an independent researcher to understand and reproduce the work.
- Document datasets, sources, preprocessing procedures, and selection criteria.
- Specify hardware, software, libraries, versions, and relevant configurations.
- Document experimental parameters and random seeds where applicable.
- Explain the selection of baselines and evaluation metrics.
- Document exclusions, failed experiments, and deviations from the original methodology.
- Distinguish training, validation, and testing procedures.
- Identify potential data leakage, sampling bias, and evaluation limitations.
- Provide code, datasets, and supplementary materials when possible and appropriate.
- Never claim reproducibility without sufficient methodological detail.
- Ensure the reported methodology matches the implementation actually used.

---

## 10. Structure and Formatting

- Maintain a consistent heading hierarchy.
- Use uniform heading capitalization, typography, and spacing.
- Maintain consistent paragraph spacing and alignment.
- Follow a standardized company research-report template.
- Ensure the abstract accurately summarizes the problem, methodology, findings, and contribution.
- Ensure the introduction establishes the motivation, problem, and research gap.
- Keep related work focused on relevant existing research.
- Keep methodology, results, and discussion clearly distinguishable.
- Include a limitations section where appropriate.
- Ensure the conclusion reflects actual findings.
- Include publication date, report version, authorship, and organizational attribution.
- Maintain consistent formatting between website and PDF versions.
- Avoid unnecessary subsection fragmentation.
- Ensure the title is specific, informative, and representative of the research.

---

## 11. Company Publication and Transparency

- Publish reports under the company's official research or publications section.
- Clearly identify the report title, authors, contributors, organizational affiliation, and publication date.
- Assign each report a unique, persistent URL.
- Maintain version numbers and revision histories.
- Distinguish research reports, technical notes, preliminary findings, and opinion pieces.
- Disclose relevant funding, sponsorship, institutional support, and conflicts of interest.
- Clearly state whether the research has undergone external peer review.
- Do not imply certification, endorsement, or independent validation without evidence.
- Provide appropriate licensing and copyright information.
- Ensure third-party materials are used with appropriate permissions and attribution.
- Preserve previously published versions or document substantial revisions.
- Provide a channel for research-related correspondence.
- Clearly distinguish company-authored findings from external research.

---

## 12. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Do not switch terminology for stylistic variation.
- Do not repeat the abstract in the introduction.
- Do not turn the introduction into an unnecessarily long literature review.
- Do not describe results without interpreting their significance.
- Do not introduce unexplained technical concepts.
- Do not overload paragraphs with citations.
- Do not use figures as substitutes for explaining important findings.
- Do not leave figures, tables, or equations unreferenced.
- Do not use inconsistent abbreviations or variable names.
- Do not introduce new findings in the conclusion without discussing them earlier.
- Do not hide methodological weaknesses behind vague language.
- Do not confuse proposed improvements with demonstrated improvements.
- Do not use unnecessarily long captions.
- Do not use unverified AI-generated citations or factual claims.
- Do not use preprints as final references.
- Do not make claims of novelty without checking relevant published literature.

---

## 13. Final Editorial and Quality Assurance

- Review the report from the perspective of an independent researcher unfamiliar with the project.
- Verify that the problem, novelty, methodology, and findings are understandable.
- Cross-check numerical results against original experimental outputs.
- Verify consistency between the abstract, main text, figures, tables, and conclusion.
- Perform a complete citation and reference audit, including publication-status verification.
- Check mathematical expressions, symbols, and terminology.
- Perform grammar, spelling, punctuation, and readability checks.
- Check figure and table numbering and cross-references.
- Verify all hyperlinks and supplementary material links.
- Review the report on desktop and mobile displays.
- Remove tracked changes, internal comments, placeholders, and drafting notes.
- Obtain an independent internal technical review before publication.
- Ensure the final report meets all mandatory company editorial standards.

---

## 14. Mandatory Publication Quality Gate

A report must pass all three categories before publication:

### Category 1: Technical Integrity
- [ ] All major claims are supported by evidence or formal citations.
- [ ] Experimental results have been cross-checked against raw execution logs.
- [ ] Methodology, constraints, and limitations are fully documented.

### Category 2: Editorial Integrity
- [ ] Formal impersonal writing (no "we", "I", "our") and terminology are consistent.
- [ ] All figures, tables, and equations are explicitly referenced in the text.
- [ ] References are verified peer-reviewed publications and contain **no preprints**.

### Category 3: Publication Integrity
- [ ] Authorship and organizational attribution are accurate.
- [ ] Publication status, licensing, and external validation status are clearly disclosed.
- [ ] Website and downloadable PDF versions are 100% synchronized.

---

## Governing Editorial Principles

1. **Objectivity over persuasion**: Reports must communicate findings, not sell conclusions.
2. **Evidence over assertion**: Every important claim must have a defensible basis.
3. **Consistency over stylistic variation**: Technical terminology must remain uniform.
4. **Clarity over complexity**: Formal writing should be precise, not unnecessarily complicated.
5. **Transparency over presentation**: Limitations, failures, and uncertainty must be disclosed.
6. **Published evidence over preliminary claims**: Final references must point to formally published sources under the company's no-preprint policy.
7. **Reproducibility over authority**: A report should be understandable and independently verifiable regardless of the company's reputation.

> **Standard**: Every report published by the company must be treated as a permanent scientific record, not merely a technical blog post or project showcase.
